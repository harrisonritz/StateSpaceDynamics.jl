# Inverse control for a system that may not be optimizing

`parameterization.md` asks which parameterization recovers a controller best,
on the premise that the data were generated by one. This document drops that
premise, because brains are at best approximately control-like, and asks the
question that replaces it:

> Not "what controller is this?" but "is this control-like at all, and how far
> from optimal?" — with the answer reported as a number rather than assumed by
> the choice of model.

That inverts the role of the costate slack. In `README.md` it is a nuisance to
be tuned away; here it is **the estimand**. Everything below follows from taking
that seriously.

Numbers are produced by `biological.jl`, which imports nothing from `src/`.

```console
$ julia --project=. docs/dev/lqr/biological.jl
$ julia --project=. docs/dev/lqr/biological.jl --only=residuals
```

## Verdict

The mixed-coordinate model **can** be given an approximate-optimality reading,
but **not with the noise model it currently has**, and the reason is sharper
than a modelling preference:

> `Σ_λλ` is not a measure of suboptimality. On data from an *exactly optimal*
> feedback controller with plant noise, the costate residual it models is
> already large — and it is large in a way that swamps genuine suboptimality.

Measured below: an exactly optimal agent with plant noise produces a mean
adjoint residual of `6.66e3`; adding substantial suboptimality on top moves it
to `6.64e3`, i.e. by nothing. Fitting a white `Σ_λλ` to that and reading it as
"how far from optimal" reports the plant noise.

The fix is not a better prior on `Σ_λλ`. It is to notice that **"the KKT
conditions" is not one hypothesis but at least two**, and they fail differently.

## 1. Three relations, and which survive which noise

For a noiseless optimal agent these are equivalent:

```math
\text{(i) } \lambda_t = P_t x_t + g_t
\qquad
\text{(ii) } \lambda_t = Q_t x_t + A^\top\lambda_{t+1} + f_t
\qquad
\text{(iii) } x_{t+1} = A x_t - S\lambda_{t+1} + c_t
```

(i) is the **Riccati graph** — the costate as a feedback readout of the current
state. (ii) is the **adjoint recursion** — the costate as a backward accumulation
along a trajectory. They are the same statement only when nothing perturbs the
trajectory.

**Under plant noise, with an agent that re-optimizes each step, they come
apart.** Substituting the closed-loop step `x_{t+1} = W_{t+1}(A x_t + ε_t)`,
`W_{t+1} = (I + S P_{t+1})^{-1}`, into (ii) and using the Riccati equation, the
`x_t` terms cancel exactly and what is left is

```math
\underbrace{\lambda_t - Q_t x_t - A^\top \lambda_{t+1}}_{\text{adjoint residual}}
= -\,A^\top P_{t+1}(I + S P_{t+1})^{-1}\,\varepsilon_t
\;=\; N_t\,\varepsilon_t .
```

So the adjoint residual is a **deterministic linear image of the plant noise**,
not an independent innovation. Measured (`n = 4`, `T = 30`, 200 trials, mean
residual norm per transition):

| generating agent | (i) Riccati | (ii) adjoint | (iii) state | `‖adj − N·state‖ / ‖adj‖` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| exactly optimal, no noise | 0 | 5.6e-13 | 7.5e-17 | — |
| **plant noise, feedback agent** | **0** | **6.66e3** | 0.0377 | **3.5e-16** |
| costate slack only (suboptimal) | 0.944 | 2.39 | 6.6e-17 | 1 (no relation) |
| both | 0.942 | 6.64e3 | 0.0375 | 3.6e-4 |

Read the first two columns across the last two rows. **The Riccati residual is
exactly blind to plant noise and responds only to suboptimality (0 → 0.944).
The adjoint residual responds overwhelmingly to plant noise and is unmoved by
suboptimality (6.66e3 → 6.64e3).** The identity is confirmed to `3.5e-16`.

Three consequences, all of which decide design questions below.

1. **`Σ_λλ` in the current model is a plant-noise parameter wearing a
   suboptimality label.** Its contamination scales as `‖AᵀP W‖·σ_x`, so it is
   worst exactly where the control hypothesis is interesting — large costs
   relative to noise.
2. **The true mixed-coordinate residual is singular and correlated, not white
   and independent.** For a feedback agent `Cov(ε^λ) = N_t Σ_x N_tᵀ` (rank ≤ n,
   time-varying, anisotropic) and `Cov(ε^x, ε^λ) = Σ_x N_tᵀ ≠ 0`. A full-rank
   white `Σ` is misspecified; so is a block-diagonal one; so is an isotropic
   costate block.
3. **The residual that measures suboptimality cleanly is the Riccati-graph one**,
   and imposing it exactly is what the closed-loop parameterization of
   `parameterization.md` does. This is now a stronger argument than the one made
   there: it is not only better specified, it is the only one of the two whose
   slack parameter means what a neuroscientist wants it to mean.

### What this does to the biological reading

The defensible interpretation of the model is the compression claim — that
trajectories lie near a family whose local dynamics and backward sensitivities
approximately satisfy the KKT conditions of a quadratic problem — and `λ` should
carry no neural interpretation. There is a reason for that beyond caution:
**`λ`'s scale is pure gauge** (§2.1), so "the costate resembles population X"
is a claim about a quantity whose units you chose.

But the compression claim needs to say *which* relation is approximately
satisfied, because (i) and (ii) are different scientific hypotheses:

* (i) approximately holds → **feedback control**: the system re-derives its
  action from the current state, so deviations are corrected. This is the
  optimal-feedback-control account of motor behaviour.
* (ii) approximately holds → **open-loop planning**: the system commits to a
  trajectory and accumulates sensitivities along it, so deviations are not
  corrected.

These make *opposite* predictions about perturbation responses, which is the
sharpest available experimental test. Treating "the KKT conditions" as one
hypothesis throws that distinction away.

## 2. The two constraints you proposed

### 2.1 Fixing `S` — yes, with a rank caveat

**As a gauge fix it is exactly right, and better than a prior.** With a *fitted*
emission the latent basis is free, and the gauge group is `GL(n)`:
`x → Tx`, `λ → T⁻ᵀλ`, `A → TAT⁻¹`, `S → TSTᵀ`, `Q → T⁻ᵀQT⁻¹`, `C → CT⁻¹`.
Choosing `T = S^{-1/2}` sends `S → I`. Verified:

```
||T S T' - I||                            = 1.57e-16
max_t ||T Phi_t T^-1 - Phi*_t||/||Phi_t|| = 5.05e-16   (closed loop is similar)
max_t ||C Phi_t - C* Phi*_t T||           = 1.07e-18   (predictions identical)
||T^-T P_1 T^-1 - P*_1||/||P_1||          = 4.07e-19   (P transforms as a cost)
```

Nothing observable moves. And it buys more than the scalar: it cuts the gauge
group from `GL(n)` to `O(n)`, which is the group that preserves `S = I`. Two
downstream benefits: orthogonal Procrustes becomes the *complete* alignment for
the audit (`README.md` currently needs the full linear map as a fallback), and
an isotropic `Σ_λλ` becomes a meaningful statement (§2.2).

**The caveat is rank, and it is not academic.** `S = B R⁻¹ Bᵀ` has rank
`min(n, m)`. For the force-controlled point mass used here:

```
S rank 2 of 4, eigenvalues 0.0, 0.0, 0.0025, 0.0025
```

No change of basis makes a rank-2 matrix the identity. Forcing `S = I` then
**fabricates control authority in directions that have none**, and the fit will
absorb that by distorting `Q`. For a neural latent space this is the live case:
if a 10-D population is driven through a few effective channels, `S` is low
rank. So:

* estimate or choose `rank(S) = r` first — it is the number of effective control
  channels, a scientific quantity worth reporting;
* whiten within the range: `S = diag(I_r, 0)` in the chosen basis;
* report the eigenspectrum of a freely fitted `S` before freezing it.

**On "fit a correlation matrix": no — that is the wrong normalization.** Unit
diagonal imposes `n` constraints where the scale gauge has exactly **one**. If
you want to keep `S`'s shape and only kill the scale, use `tr S = n` or
`det S = 1`: one constraint, exactly the gauge dimension. Use `S = I` (or
`diag(I_r,0)`) when you also want to fix the basis.

**One honest warning.** Freezing `S` is only gauge-fixing when the emission is
free. With a *fixed*, physically meaningful `C`, `S` is a substantive parameter —
it says which state directions are cheap to move — and freezing it at `I` is an
assumption of isotropic full-rank control authority, which is false for most
real plants. And even after the scalar gauge is fixed, `(S, Q)` need not be
jointly identified: for a stationary closed loop the inverse-optimality set is a
cone, not a point (§3 of `parameterization.md`). Freezing `S` selects a point in
that cone. That is fine, and far better than selecting one invisibly through
`q0` — but it should be reported as a choice, not a normalization.

### 2.2 Block `Σ` with a scaled-identity costate block — right count, wrong centre

Three separate claims are bundled here; they do not all survive.

**Block-diagonal (no state–costate cross-covariance): wrong for the hypothesis
you want to test.** §1 shows the true cross-block for a feedback agent is
`Σ_x N_tᵀ ≠ 0`, and the joint residual is *singular* — the costate residual is a
deterministic function of the state residual. Imposing block-diagonality asserts
that the costate slack is *not* plant noise fed through the value function,
which is exactly the thing that needs testing rather than assuming. It is a
defensible simplification, but it is a modelling commitment, not a
regularization.

**Isotropic `σ_ν² I` instead of a free `n(n+1)/2` matrix: right, and the
parameter count is right** — `README.md` shows the free matrix inflates until
the state stops being an LQR state, and a scalar has far more leverage. Two
corrections:

1. **Isotropy is basis-dependent.** Under `x → Tx`, `Σ_λλ → T⁻ᵀΣ_λλT⁻¹`, so
   `σ²I` is not preserved by `GL(n)`. Verified: `‖Oᵀ(σ²I)O − σ²I‖ = 1.4e-15`
   for orthogonal `O`, but `19.3` for a general `M`. **So your two suggestions
   are not independent — isotropy only means something once `S` is fixed**,
   because that is what reduces the gauge group to `O(n)`. Adopt them together
   or neither.
2. **Centre it on the derived value, not on zero.** §1 gives the decomposition
   `ε^λ_t = N_t ε^x_t + ν_t`. The first term is what an *exactly optimal* agent
   produces; only `ν_t` is suboptimality. Modelling `ε^λ` as `σ_ν²I` about zero
   attributes the first term to the second.

**Fixing `Σ_λλ` at `1e-4 I`: no.** Beyond the two points above, `Σ_λλ` is **not
gauge-invariant**: under `(λ, S, Q) → (cλ, c⁻¹S, cQ)` it scales as `c²`. The
number `1e-4` has no content until the gauge is fixed, which is why the harness
finds it has to be retuned for every configuration. Once the gauge *is* fixed,
you should **fit** the scalar rather than pin it, because it is the quantity the
science is about.

**Report a dimensionless index.** For cross-dataset comparison use

```math
\rho^2 \;=\; \frac{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma_{\lambda\lambda}}
                  {\operatorname{tr}\!\left(P\,\Sigma_{xx}P^\top\right)},
```

the costate slack relative to the costate variation the Riccati map itself
predicts. Numerator and denominator both scale as `c²` under the scale gauge and
are both `O(n)`-invariant, so `ρ` is comparable across fits, subjects and tasks
in a way that `Σ_λλ` is not.

## 3. The equality-constrained KKT proposal

The document is technically strong and I checked its algebra rather than its
prose. The local substitution and Schur recursion in its §5 are correct: with
`s_t = [x_t; x_{t+1}; λ_{t+1}]`, the maps `T_t = [I 0 0; Q_k 0 Aᵀ]` and
`R_t = [−A I S]` are right, and the canonical updates
`J = TᵀHT + RᵀW⁻¹R`, `g = Tᵀ(h − Hd) + RᵀW⁻¹c`, and the Schur message
`H^msg = J_zz − J_zx J_xx⁻¹ J_xz` are the standard constrained-Gaussian
elimination, correctly derived. It is a genuine `O(Tn³)` replacement for the
dense `(dT)²` sampler.

### What to take regardless of backend

* **§4, the measure convention.** Correct and easy to get catastrophically
  wrong. When `C_θ` depends on fitted parameters, a `δ(C_θ z − d_θ)` density is
  ambiguous up to row scaling, and different scalings change the estimated `A`
  and `Q`. Their resolution — declare `x_{1:T}` the free coordinate and `λ`
  deterministic — is the right one. Adopt it verbatim.
* **§11, three distinct terminal concepts.** The most valuable section. The KKT
  boundary, a soft boundary misspecification, and *outcome selection* are
  genuinely different, and the current `condition_terminal` conflates the first
  and the third. For biology this is not pedantry: if you analyze only
  successful reaches, you have a selection effect that is not a terminal cost,
  and the normalizer differs.
* **§10**, the Laplace determinant restricted to the `nT` free coordinates
  rather than `2nT` — correct, and easy to get wrong in a way that silently
  doubles an entropy.
* **§9**, LDLᵀ or sparse QR rather than Cholesky on an indefinite KKT matrix —
  correct.
* **§12**, that `S = BR⁻¹Bᵀ` cannot separate `B`, `R`, or the control dimension,
  and that measured actions should be modelled explicitly — correct and
  important; it is also the cleanest route to pinning `rank(S)` from §2.1.
* **§3**, no independent prior on `λ₁`. Correct, and under hard constraints it
  is *forced*: `λ₁` is determined by `x_{1:T}`, so a prior on it would be a
  disguised constraint on the state trajectory, counted twice.
* **§15.2**, that raw likelihoods do not converge in the soft→hard limit without
  subtracting the diverging normalizer of the collapsing dimensions — correct
  and subtle.
* **§17**, the decision criteria. Appropriately skeptical, and the list of
  things that do *not* support the model is the right list.

### Where I disagree

**1. It imposes the wrong exact constraint for the default biological
hypothesis.** The hard constraint in §2 and §5 is the *adjoint recursion*, (ii).
§1 shows that relation is violated by `N_t ε_t` for any agent that re-optimizes
under plant noise — by `6.66e3` against a Riccati residual of exactly `0` in the
measured configuration. So the proposed backend imposes as exact the one of the
two relations that a feedback controller breaks. It is the correct constraint for
a **deterministic plant** or an **open-loop planner**, and that is a real
hypothesis worth fitting — but it should be named as such, not adopted as *the*
hard KKT model.

**2. The right constraint needs almost none of this machinery.** Substituting
(i) into (iii) gives `(I + S P_{t+1}) x_{t+1} = A x_t + c_t − S g_{t+1} + ε_t` —
a **forward Markov chain in `x`**, handled by an ordinary Kalman filter.
Substituting (ii) makes `x_{t+1}` depend on `x_{t+1:T}`, which is *why* the
constrained message pass is needed. So the proposal builds sophisticated
machinery for the constraint that requires it, while the constraint that is
probably right for motor control requires none. That is a large difference in
cost: six milestones, a new type, constrained IRLS and a reverse pass through
Schur complements, against a Riccati sweep and one extra scalar.

**3. "EM cannot be reused" is overstated, and the stated reason is wrong.**
§6 argues the posterior at `θ_old` lives on a manifold that `θ_new` violates, so
the expected complete-data log density is `−∞`. That is true of naive EM in `z`
coordinates. But the constraint *solves* for `λ`: unrolling (ii) with the
terminal condition gives

```math
\lambda_t = \sum_{s=t}^{T-1}(A^\top)^{s-t}\!\left(Q_{k(s)}x_s + f_s\right)
          + (A^\top)^{T-t}\!\left(Q_{k_T}x_T + f_T\right),
```

a *linear* function of `x_{1:T}`. In the reduced coordinate the complete-data
density is an ordinary Gaussian in `x` and `Q(θ | θ_old) = E_{q(x)}[\log p(x, y
| θ)]` is perfectly well defined and monotone. What is actually true is weaker
and more useful: **the M-step is no longer closed-form**, because `λ_θ(x)`
depends on `θ` through `(A, Q_k)` nonlinearly. Their *recommendation* — optimize
the constrained marginal likelihood directly — is still right, because a
nonconvex M-step inside an EM loop buys little. But the wrong reason would also
rule out valid hybrid schemes, including the imputation E-step recommended in
`parameterization.md` §3.5.

**4. The migration `W = Σ[1:n,1:n]` will bias `W`.** §1 gives the true residual
structure for a feedback agent: the state block of a fitted `Σ` is contaminated
by the correlated costate block, so it is not a clean estimate of the state
residual covariance.

**5. The staging is inverted.** §1's ladder starts at "hard terminal and adjoint
relations" and softens. For a system that is approximately optimal at best, a
hard constraint asserts a relation holds *exactly*, and every violation must be
absorbed by `W`, by the observation noise, or by distorting `Q`. Start at the
softest model with a **fitted** slack and tighten; use the hard model as the
`σ_ν → 0` limit for a likelihood-ratio test, not as the default. The slack is
the measurement — do not begin by setting it to zero.

**6. It inherits the switching difficulty.** Keeping the `2n` latent keeps the
`γ`-versus-cost conflict documented in `parameterization.md` §2.3, where the
closed-loop parameterization makes the regime comparison an ordinary one between
two `n`-dimensional Gaussians.

## 4. What is actually measurable

Encouraging conclusions about suboptimality need to survive the question of
whether the slack is identified at all. Measured, 400 trials, `T = 30`,
observation noise `0.006`, one scalar fitted jointly with the plant noise:

| slack model | emission | true `σ_ν` | fit `σ_ν` | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Riccati-graph slack, `Ω_t = W(σ_x²I + σ_ν² SSᵀ)Wᵀ` | position only | 1.0 | 6 (grid max) | **not identified** |
| Riccati-graph slack | full state | 1.0 | 6 (grid max) | **not identified** |
| max-ent control noise, `Ω_t = σ_x²I + β⁻¹B(R+BᵀPB)⁻¹Bᵀ` | position only | 1.0 | 1.95 | not identified |
| max-ent control noise | **full state** | 1.0 | **1.026** | **recovered** |

The Riccati-slack rows are worth reading carefully: the fit pegs at a *grid
boundary*, and **which** boundary depends on the grid — a narrower sweep of the
same data puts it at `σ_ν = 0` instead of 6. The profile is flat, so the argmax
is an artifact of where the search stops. That is stronger evidence of
non-identifiability than any single fitted number would be, and it is the
failure mode to watch for in real fits: a suboptimality estimate that moves when
you change the optimizer's bounds is not a measurement.

Two structural lessons, and one caution about how much to read into this.

**Suboptimality lives in `range(B)`.** Every slack model puts its variance in
the directions control acts through — `SSᵀ` and `B Ξ Bᵀ` have the same range. In
this plant that is the velocity subspace, which a position-only emission never
sees. **If the emission does not span the control directions, suboptimality is
not identified at any sample size.** For neural data that is the design
question: does the recorded population span the directions that drive behaviour?

**Plant noise and slack compete in the same slot,** so what could separate them
is shape or time course. `σ_x²I` is isotropic and constant; `SSᵀ` and `BΞ_tBᵀ`
are rank-`r`. In the Riccati-graph model both terms are sandwiched by the *same*
`W_{t+1}`, so they share a time course and differ only in shape. `BΞ_tBᵀ` is
additionally time-varying through `P_{t+1}`.

That difference looked decisive in the table above. **It is not.** The table
compares the two models at the same *nominal* scalar, which is nowhere near the
same observable effect: at `σ_ν = 1` the max-ent term carries 86 % of the
innovation variance and the Riccati term carries 0.02 %. Sweeping the magnitude
instead, and scoring on the share the data can actually see,

```math
\varphi = \frac{\text{slack contribution to the innovation variance}}{\text{total innovation variance}},
```

| model | emission | true `φ` → | 0.003 | 0.025 | 0.14 | 0.35 | 0.86 | 0.98 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Riccati slack | position only | fit `φ` | 0.42 | 0.42 | 0.42 | — | — | — |
| Riccati slack | full state | fit `φ` | 0.015 | 0.038 | **0.166** | — | — | — |
| max-ent noise | position only | fit `φ` | — | — | — | 0.955 | 0.967 | 0.987 |
| max-ent noise | full state | fit `φ` | — | — | — | 0.461 | **0.854** | **0.982** |

**At matched `φ` the max-ent tying has no advantage in this design** — the
Riccati slack recovers `φ = 0.14` to within 19 %, while max-ent recovers
`φ = 0.35` only to within 32 %. The earlier row was a power artifact, and the
correct reading is simpler than "time course":

* **what governs detectability is `φ`, and whether the emission spans
  `range(B)`** — not which slack model is used;
* with a full-state emission this design (400 trials, `T = 30`, observation
  noise 0.006) resolves `φ ≳ 0.15` to about 20 % and `φ ≳ 0.85` to about 5 %;
* with a position-only emission nothing is resolved until the slack almost
  completely dominates (`φ > 0.98`).

The max-ent tying is still worth having — it removes a free parameter and reads
as an inverse temperature — but **it is not what makes suboptimality
measurable.** Observing the control directions is.

Run `biological.jl --only=threshold` on your own configuration before concluding
that a fitted `ρ` near zero means "optimal" rather than "underpowered".

## 5. Revised ladder and recommendation

Order by hypothesis, not only by hardness. Every level is a state-space model on
`x` alone except level 4.

| level | what is imposed | hypothesis | machinery |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | free `Φ_t` per condition | no shared structure | LDS |
| 1 | `Φ_t` free but **shared** across targets/conditions | structure without optimality | LDS + tying |
| 2 | `Φ_t = A − B K_t(θ)` from a Riccati sweep | exact feedback optimality | Kalman + Riccati |
| **3** | **level 2 + slack `ν ~ N(0, σ_ν² I)`, `σ_ν` fitted** | **approximate feedback optimality** | **level 2 + one scalar** |
| 3′ | level 3 with `Ξ_t = [β(R + BᵀP_{t+1}B)]⁻¹` | bounded rationality, identifiable slack | same |
| 4 | hard adjoint + hard terminal on `(x, λ)` | open-loop planning / deterministic plant | constrained KKT pass |
| 5 | soft adjoint, white `Σ_λλ` (current model) | — | current |

Level 5 is the awkward one, and §1 says why: its slack parameter mixes two
mechanisms with opposite meanings, so its fitted value answers neither question.

**Recommended order of work.**

1. **Build level 3.** It is level 2 of `parameterization.md` plus one scalar:
   the Riccati sweep with the closed-form adjoint of §3.1 there, a Kalman
   filter, and `σ_ν`. Days, not months, and it reuses the emissions, `SLDS`
   layer, grouping, priors and holdout scoring already in `src/`.
2. **Adopt the `S` gauge with an explicit rank**, per §2.1, and report `Q` in
   the whitened basis. Report `rank(S)` as a result.
3. **Report `ρ`, not `Σ_λλ`**, and run the threshold sweep for your design
   before interpreting a small `ρ`.
4. **Take §4, §9, §10, §11, §12, §15 and §17 from the proposal now.** They are
   backend-independent and several are things you would otherwise get wrong.
5. **Fit level 3′ for parsimony and interpretation, not for identifiability.**
   The max-ent tying removes a parameter and reads as an inverse temperature,
   but §4 measures no detectability advantage at matched `φ`. What buys
   detectability is an emission that spans `range(B)`.
6. **Defer the constrained KKT backend until you have a reason to prefer the
   open-loop hypothesis** — level 3 fitting badly, with residuals showing
   anticipatory rather than corrective structure. Then build it, because it is
   the right machinery for that hypothesis, and invert its staging: soft first,
   hard as the limiting test.

## 6. Reporting, and the test worth running

For biological data, the strongest claim the machinery supports is the
compression claim, stated with its gauges:

> The dynamics are consistent with a control-like KKT organization of this
> family, up to the stated gauge and identifiable equivalence class, with
> suboptimality index `ρ = …` against a detection threshold of `…` for this
> design.

Two things worth more than parameter RMSE.

**Perturbation responses.** (i) and (ii) predict *opposite* behaviour after a
perturbation — correction versus accumulation. If the experiment can perturb,
that is a direct test between the two hypotheses, and it does not depend on any
gauge.

**The minimum-intervention signature, as a cross-constraint.** An optimal
feedback controller corrects deviations only in task-relevant directions, so
trial-to-trial variability should be large in the null space of `Q` and small in
its range — with the closed-loop covariance following
`Σ_{t+1} = Φ_t Σ_t Φ_tᵀ + Ω_t`. On its own this is not discriminating, because a
free LDS fits any covariance. It becomes discriminating as a *cross-constraint*:
the same `Q` must simultaneously explain the mean trajectories, the feedback
gains, and the anisotropy of the variability — three different functionals of one
parameter, across conditions. A free LDS has separate parameters for each and
gets no credit for the agreement. That is the cheapest strong evidence available
and it should sit near the top of the ladder, not at the bottom.
